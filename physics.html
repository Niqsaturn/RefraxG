Propagation physics: from an equation to a field

The observatory uses calculations to represent possible physical behavior. A scientific simulation begins with a source, a medium, geometry, boundary conditions and units. A solution can be exact within an ideal model and still be a poor prediction of a real environment if its inputs are inaccurate.

Wavelength and power

For a wave of frequency f and phase velocity v, the wavelength is λ = v/f. Electromagnetic waves in vacuum travel at c = 299,792,458 m/s. Sound in air is much slower and depends on the thermodynamic state. The same frequency does not imply the same wavelength in different physical domains.

An isotropic free-space reference with equivalent isotropic radiated power P gives power flux density S = P/(4πr²), in watts per square metre, at range r. In the plane-wave far field in vacuum, the RMS electric field is E = √(Z₀S), where Z₀ is approximately 376.73 ohms. These relationships do not solve the near field of an arbitrary antenna.

Why line of sight is not enough

Terrain may obstruct part of the first Fresnel zone even if the straight line between source and receiver remains clear. An approximate diffraction model considers signed clearance relative to the wavelength and the distances from the obstacle to both endpoints. Multiple obstacles, irregular geometry and material properties can introduce additional uncertainty.

Material interaction

At a dielectric interface, some energy may reflect, transmit or be absorbed. Conductivity and complex permittivity depend on frequency and material state. Finite slabs can also exhibit interference between internal reflections. For a passive model, the calculated reflected, transmitted and absorbed fractions must satisfy conservation of energy within the numerical tolerance.

Interference and inverse mathematics

For coherent fields with known relative phase, amplitudes can add constructively or destructively. If phase is unknown, a deterministic cancellation pattern cannot be asserted. Independent frequencies or incoherent sources generally require power-domain combination. An inverse calculation asks which physical parameters could explain the available observations; multiple candidates can fit the same data. The uncertainty depends on sensor calibration, geometry and the forward model.

Atmospheric and acoustic context

Public ionospheric products estimate quantities such as total electron content and critical frequency. They do not measure the local reception of a particular broadcast. Acoustic moving-source scenarios require a separate propagation speed and source assumptions; aircraft position alone cannot recover an acoustic waveform. The application makes these distinctions visible so that calculated conditions are not mistaken for recorded sound.
