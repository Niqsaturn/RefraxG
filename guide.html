Exploring the invisible environment

Refrax G is an interactive field observatory for exploring physical models and public environmental reference information. It brings a geographic interface together with radio propagation, material interactions, atmospheric context and deterministic simulations. A map can help connect equations to a real place, but it cannot establish that a signal was received there without suitable measurements.

Start with the live observatory. Open the map, choose a global layer and zoom into a region of interest. Every visual layer has a different scientific meaning.

Navigate the globe

Start with the planetary overview to compare broad spatial patterns. Zoom toward a city to inspect available buildings and terrain. Use the layer controls to isolate a single field when overlapping colors make interpretation difficult. The underlying map describes published geographic geometry; the wave visualizations describe calculated or conditional scenarios. An apparent intersection is not proof of physical interference.

Scientific workspaces

Field Atlas

Inspect global reference sites, environmental context, mapped geometry and field layers. When zooming in, the map may switch to more detailed representations. Missing data should remain unknown, not be treated as zero.

Spectral laboratory

Explore the distinction between frequency, wavelength, power and observed spectra. A spectral peak can have many explanations, and a synthetic waveform cannot establish the content of an unknown real transmission.

Geometry and field simulation

Use explicit inputs to evaluate a propagation scenario or an inverse problem. A bearing, a timing difference or a power reading each constrains the solution differently. The number, placement and calibration of sensors determine whether a solution is meaningful.

Wave synthesis and experimental materials

Compare hypothetical sources and materials under specified frequencies, positions and physical properties. Keep coherent phase calculations separate from independent-source power sums, and distinguish idealized material behavior from a measurement of a real structure.

Choose your location

Browser location access is optional. You can instead use map selection or enter coordinates. A location selects the geographic context for your session; it does not identify an emitter or establish that signals are being measured nearby. Public catalog density and modeled fields must be interpreted within the coverage of their sources.
